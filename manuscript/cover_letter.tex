To the Editor-in-Chief, \emph{Science and Medicine in Football}

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, \emph{``Playing time tracks recent exposure in
fifteen men's and women's football leagues: the denominator gradient and what
it does to per-minute injury rates''}, as an Original Investigation.

\textbf{What the paper shows.} Football injury incidence is conventionally
reported per 1000 hours of exposure. We show that the denominator of that rate
is not neutral: recorded minutes rise and fall with the workload under study,
so the division removes part of the association it is meant to scale. In our
reference cohort it removed two thirds of the excess association, moving a rate
ratio of 1.27 to 1.09 with nothing in the model output to show for it.

\textbf{Why this journal.} The readership of \emph{Science and Medicine in
Football} is the readership that computes this rate, and the journal's stated
mission covers methodological approaches as well as practice. The evidence is
multi-league and multi-sex rather than single-club: fifteen European first
tiers, 658,286 appearances, eight men's competitions across eight seasons and
seven women's competitions from two independent data providers.

\textbf{Precedent in your pages.} The topic and the level of technicality are
both already the journal's own. \emph{Science and Medicine in Football} has
published statistical-methodology work on injury-risk analysis with no new
cohort of its own --- Bache-Mathiesen et al.\ on handling missing data in
training-load and injury research (2022;6(4):452--464) --- analytics-driven
injury-risk work in its current volume --- Hecksteden et al., ``How to mitigate
injury risk after return-to-play in elite football? Expert judgement meets data
analytics'' (2026) --- and women's-football injury surveillance --- Mkumbuzi et
al.\ on the 2020 COSAFA Women's Championship (2023;7(1):74--80). We placed this
submission against those articles, and we wrote its statistics to the journal's
own published recommendations (Impellizzeri, Meyer and Wagenpfeil,
2019;3(1):1--2): estimates with intervals throughout, every verdict read from
interval bounds, and no reliance on significance language.

\textbf{This is not descriptive match analytics.} It is a measurement-validity
study for injury surveillance, and its output is a check a practitioner can run
on data they already hold before any model is fitted. The check needs an
appearance table only --- player identifier, date, recorded minutes --- and no
injury data of any kind.

\textbf{Sex and gender.} Results are measured and reported separately for men's
and women's competitions, and neither population is pooled into the other. The
outcome analysis is confined to men's football, and the manuscript gives
measured reasons rather than an assertion: a matched audit of the two public
injury records, applying one sampling frame and one parser to both, is reported
in Table 3. We would rather withhold a women's incidence analysis than publish
one that describes the recording regime instead of the players.

\textbf{Transparency in place of pre-registration.} The study was not
pre-registered, and the manuscript says so plainly. In compensation, all
analysis code is public, the data are deposited, the complete original output
of the statistical software for all thirty reported models is supplied as
Appendix A, and an automated test suite at 100\% statement and branch coverage
recomputes every number in the manuscript from the deposited tables and fails
when text and data disagree.

\textbf{Preprint.} This work is available on arXiv as arXiv:2608.11228
(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11228}). We understand from your instructions
that prior posting does not preclude consideration and that anonymity cannot be
guaranteed for a manuscript with a preprint. One point of candour: the version
posted at the time of writing (v2) predates the fifteen-league extension and
states that the within-starter pattern held without exception. This manuscript
supersedes that claim --- the restriction fails in Spain and Sweden --- and a
replacement has already been submitted as a new version of the same record,
so that no reader has to reconcile two versions on their own.

\textbf{Declarations.} The research received no specific grant from any funding
agency in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors. The authors report
there are no competing interests to declare. Generative AI use is declared in
the manuscript. The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and
all authors have read and approved the submitted version. The manuscript is
within the 4,500-word Original Investigation limit, with an unstructured
abstract inside the 250-word limit, and reporting follows STROBE with the
completed checklist supplied.

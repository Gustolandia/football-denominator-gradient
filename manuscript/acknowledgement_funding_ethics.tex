{\bfseries Confirmation of ethical compliance}

The study reused exclusively public, pre-existing records. There was no
recruitment, no contact with any individual, no intervention, and no access to
private medical records or clinical files. No primary data were collected from
human participants, so informed consent was not applicable.

No institutional ethics approval number can be provided, and we state the
reason rather than imply an approval that does not exist. Trinity College
Dublin guidance does not permit retrospective ethical approval of secondary
research on already-public data; no determination was sought before the
analysis began and none can now be issued. We are happy to supply any
correspondence with our institution that the editorial office requires.

Because the records concern identifiable professional athletes and the absence
labels are health-related, they are treated as special-category personal data
notwithstanding their public availability. Processing was for scientific
research under the research provisions of the General Data Protection
Regulation, restricted to data already published by the source, with no
attempt to obtain, infer or link clinical information. No deposited file
carries a player's name, the data provider's player identifier, or a match
date; the hand-built audit records are keyed by surrogates drawn at random and
held apart from the archive, and row-level model diagnostics are not
deposited. We describe this as de-identification of the archive rather than
anonymisation of the individuals, because the sampling rule is published.

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been
published previously except as a preprint (arXiv:2608.11228). All authors have
read and approved the submitted version.

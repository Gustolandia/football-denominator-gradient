# Public-data v4 scientific audit

## Current publication role

This file preserves the scientific audit of the v4 national-exposure
extension. It no longer defines the main manuscript hierarchy. The controlling
publication analysis is generated by `src/36_jsams_second_referee_analysis.py`
in `jsams_revised_claim_hierarchy.csv` and documented in
`manuscript/manuscript.tex` and `manuscript/supplement.tex`.

The manuscript is now a public-data measurement study. Its reference estimand
is the probability of a same-day public report per recorded appearance, not a
per-minute clinical injury rate. National chronology is supplementary because
adding senior competitive national minutes changed 2.35% of eligible rows and
did not change the inference. The national-source checks support part of the
chronology only; they do not validate the reported-event outcome or measure
national training.

## Audit decision

The corrected v4 extension does not overturn the seven-day total-burden
conclusion. Adding observed senior competitive national-team minutes changed
2,379/101,042 eligible club-match rows (2.35%; 95% CI 2.26--2.45), including
53 proxy-event rows, and moved 2,171 rows from zero club burden to positive
combined burden. The global burden-by-history interaction was `p = 0.664`
with club exposure and `p = 0.664` after adding senior competitive national
exposure. The largest absolute shift among selected predictions was 4.3%.

Recent national participation also produced no multiplicity-adjusted signal.
The seven-day higher-history IRR was 1.29 (95% CI 0.83--2.03; Holm `p =
1.000`) with observed minutes and 1.50 (0.96--2.35; Holm `p = 1.000`) with
fixed 90-minute exposure. Squad-only status was too sparse for adjusted
inference. These are Tier 5 sensitivities, not positive findings.

Independent CC0 sources reconstructed 90.5% (89.4--91.5) of identified senior
competitive matches, and matched World Cup records gave 96.9% (95.8--97.7)
normalised player agreement. This supports part of the chronology, not complete
national exposure. Under the current manuscript hierarchy it is a Tier 5
secondary sensitivity because it changes neither the reference outcome nor the
main measurement interpretation.

## Core integrity correction before v4 fitting

The v4 raw acquisition remains immutable, but all exposure scopes were rerun
on the same corrected core panel. The old player calendar stopped at each
player's first and last EPL appearance and could omit valid cup, European, or
other EPL-club-season appearances. The old history build also omitted
strictly prior records before panel entry. Preserving those defects in a
"frozen" comparator would make the reference internally invalid.

The retained appearances covered 1,220 cohort players and included 17,491
senior competitive rows (1,262,280 minutes), 10,052 senior-friendly rows
(631,559 minutes), and 5,538 youth/Olympic rows (389,598 minutes). Every
retained in-window row came from cached player-performance histories; the bulk
published snapshot supplied no in-window appearances for this cohort.

Independent schedule reconstruction covered 2,785/3,077 identified senior
competitive matches (90.5%, 95% CI 89.4--91.5). In the World Cup audit
subset, normalized player agreement was 1,276/1,317 (96.9%, 95% CI
95.8--97.7). The revision-defined official-source gate remains unexecuted
because the generated ledger retained blank official URLs and identifiers for
all 3,077 candidate games; consequently none was marked verified. This is not
evidence that 3,077 official records disagreed. All expanded-exposure models
remain `sensitivity_only`.

## Exposure changes and model comparison

For the seven-day window, senior competitive national minutes changed 2,379
rows and 53 event rows. Among changed rows, the median increment was 90
minutes, the 90th percentile was 167, and the maximum was 240. Including all
senior internationals changed 3,212 rows; the broader youth/Olympic scope
changed 3,401. The senior competitive chronology shortened 4,739/101,042
recorded recovery intervals (4.69%).

The refreshed strict-club reconstruction differed from the stored all-club
burden on 610/101,042 rows, with a maximum difference of 90 minutes, because
the all-club definition includes recorded club friendlies. Direct models
therefore retain the canonical all-club burden and add only national
increments.

| Exposure scope | Global spline-by-history interaction p-value |
| --- | ---: |
| Club all-competition burden | 0.664 |
| Club plus senior competitive national burden | 0.664 |
| Club plus all senior national burden | 0.562 |
| Club plus broader international burden | 0.541 |

No exposure-dependent v4 contrast survived Holm adjustment. The supported
zero-minute history contrast is a baseline prior-history difference, not an
effect of national or congested exposure. The extension therefore shows that
the observed national minutes do not explain the club-burden result; it cannot
show that unobserved international or training exposure is irrelevant.

## National-status sensitivity

The status analysis distinguishes an observed national appearance from a
recorded squad-only state. In the seven-day observed-minute model, recent
participation gave IRRs of 1.20 (0.83--1.72) in intermediate-history rows and
1.29 (0.83--2.03) in higher-history rows. Their ratio was 1.08 (0.61--1.91).
The corresponding fixed-90 higher-history IRR was 1.50 (0.96--2.35), and all
Holm-adjusted p-values were 1.000. Squad-only rows had only three
higher-history events and failed the support gate, so no adjusted squad-only
estimate is reported. Squad status does not measure national-team training.

## Type-history interpretation audit

The current 88,573-row type-history analysis does not use the significance of
one estimate and non-significance of another as evidence that the estimates
differ. After matched recency, the mutually exclusive continuous
muscle/tendon and type-discordant comparator IRRs are 1.041 and 1.040 per
additional prior report per 10,000 previous club minutes; their direct ratio
is 1.00 (95% CI 0.95--1.06). In the formal model the corresponding values are
1.035 and 1.024, with a direct ratio of 1.01 (0.97--1.06). These continuous
models do not distinguish tissue specificity, and the positive comparator
estimate of similar magnitude leaves shared reporting or player-profile bias
plausible.

The recency-adjusted binary result may instead reflect a high-frequency-tail
association or a dichotomisation artefact. The pipeline does not choose between
those readings. Among rows with a prior same-type report, frequency--log-
recency correlations are -0.361 for muscle/tendon and -0.356 for the
comparator. Exact fitted-design VIFs are 2.76 and 2.63, and the muscle/tendon
high-step log standard error changes from 0.1113 before recency to 0.1110
afterward. The attenuation is therefore not explained by variance inflation or
loss of precision.

## Post-primary current-data extension

`src/33_matchproxy_current_data_extensions.py` reran seven ordered checks on
the canonical intermediate/higher-history frame of 72,445 match rows, 1,057
players, and 1,592 proxy events. These are observational diagnostics, not v4
national-exposure estimates.

### Selection and curve stability

- Recorded lineup roles covered 53,181 rows and 1,285 events. The pooled
  lineup-role-by-spline test was unsupported (chi-square 1.10 on 4 df, `p =
  0.894`). Starter, substitute, return-excluded, and combined refits did not
  identify one stable early maximum.
- The 15--45-minute peak appeared in 3/8 intermediate-history and 0/8
  higher-history spline specifications. Substitute-list shares were 32.8%
  versus 7.2% in the early and 90-minute bands for intermediate history and
  41.6% versus 9.6% for higher history. Recent-return shares were 10.9% versus
  4.8% and 15.9% versus 7.1%.
- In 1,000 player-bootstrap refits, the early band was the global maximum in
  51.4% (48.3--54.5) of intermediate-history and 29.6% (26.9--32.5) of
  higher-history samples. Maximum locations ranged widely. The fitted feature
  is therefore composition- and specification-sensitive, not a workload
  threshold.

### Reporting and absence-date proxies

- Type text was classifiable for 1,210/1,592 events (76.0%, 95% CI
  73.8--78.0): 85.6% for same-day and 71.6% for lag-1 events.
- The reporting inverse-probability sensitivity failed its prespecified gate:
  minimum fitted probability 0.074, maximum weight 13.5, and 99th-percentile
  weight 11.2. Weighted outputs are archive-only and do not correct the main
  model.
- Dated public episodes gave 922 (bootstrap 802--1,049) and 633 (551--719)
  reported absence days per 1,000 recorded match hours in higher- and
  intermediate-history rows. These are public-date proxies, not confirmed
  days lost, clinical injury burden, or injury severity.

### Joint support, within-player association, and context

- The support-limited two-versus-one-prior-match IRR was 1.28 (0.93--1.76)
  for intermediate history and 1.47 (1.07--2.02) for higher history. Their
  ratio was 1.15 (0.82--1.60; Holm `p = 0.416`), so the isolated stratum
  estimate does not establish different exposure associations.
- Conditional player-season models associated each additional 90 prior
  minutes with odds ratios of 1.35 (1.20--1.51) and 1.42 (1.23--1.64), but
  their ratio was 1.06 (0.88--1.27). These models condition on event-containing
  player-seasons and selection into the current appearance.
- All 72,445 model rows reconciled to one source current match, with 5,061,301
  observed and source minutes. Global history interactions remained
  unsupported with current-competition adjustment (`p = 0.661`), in current
  EPL matches only (`p = 0.678`), and with player/current-match two-way
  clustering (`p = 0.657`).

## Result-tier decision

No result meets the strict Tier 1 or Tier 3 definitions. The controlling
publication registry has three Tier 2 results: the same-day per-appearance
cumulative-exposure association after the complete 63-model correction, the
independent-source outcome-attribution audit, and the link between a same-day
record and its recorded-minute denominator. These lead the abstract because
they answer the measurement-sensitivity question. The bounded selection
weighting, continuous history-interaction null, historical categorical null,
and national-exposure chronology sensitivity are Tier 5. Outcome-quality
restrictions are also Tier 5 because their stated family has no adjusted
rejection. The prior-history incidence gradient is a Tier 4 corroboration.
Type-specific recency, recovery, duration, and other historical analyses remain
secondary and are not main displays.

## Unresolved limitations

- The official full-calendar ledger remains uncompleted, so identified
  national records cannot be called a complete international chronology.
- The revised bounded selection model passes its measured overlap, weight-tail,
  and balance gates, but it is not a correction for complete roster membership,
  symptoms, medical clearance, or tactical need.
- None of 1,907 venue keys has a retained verified coordinate source, so
  travel distance and time-zone change cannot be modelled.
- The original official injury-source validation queue remains unreviewed. A
  separate 30-record publication audit is complete, source-independent, and
  exposure-blinded, but has one assessor and cannot measure missed cases.
- Training exposure, symptoms, medical clearance, tactical selection, exact
  injury onset, confirmed diagnosis, and clinical time loss remain unavailable.
